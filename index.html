Mission: landing site to Mount Sharp

Goal: follow the first leg of Curiosity's route to the foot of Mount Sharp, toward the boxwork formations studied in 2025 (not mapped here).

Radiation reference

Curiosity's RAD instrument measured an average of about 0.67 mSv/day from galactic cosmic rays on the surface at Gale (Aug 2012 to Jun 2013, near solar maximum), versus about 1.8 mSv/day in transit. Round-trip transit adds about 0.66 Sv, and 500 days at the surface rate adds about 0.34 Sv (0.67 mSv/day × 500 days), so a 500-day surface mission totals around 1 Sv. Solar storms add short-term doses. This is a reference value, not a map layer.

Source: SwRI press release on Hassler et al., Science (2013)
Known limits

Legs are straight lines between stops, not the rover's path. No slope or elevation along legs, no rock or hazard map, geologic units from a 1:20,000,000 map (regional context, not walking scale), no live space-weather data, and suit mobility is only the pace you set. Stop positions were read from NASA's labeled traverse map (within a few hundred meters).
